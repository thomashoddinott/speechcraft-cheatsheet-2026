---
title: "How do I memorise a speech?"
---

Reading from a script made me look unprepared, and learning it word for word made me sound like a tape playing in my head. What works for me is compression: three keyword bullets per paragraph, fifteen in total, on one A5 card. I rehearse from the card a couple of times a day, and the speech comes out slightly different every time, which leaves room to read the room.

The prompt I use once the speech is finished:

```
Here's my finished speech. For each paragraph, give me one line on the logical move it makes, then three 6-12 word bullets: memory triggers, not summaries. I'll rebuild sentences live. If a paragraph won't fit three, tell me.
```

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Example: my card from my speech on 21 September 2026</summary>

```
1 · Anecdote — I'm disciplined now. Took one week off.
First time leading — old Thomas couldn't have
One week off all summer, make it count
Bike bags loaded, bus to southern France

2 · The trade — But what did that cost me?
Four countries, South America, hobbies for three lifetimes
Curiosity killed the cat — only good if you stick
Competent now, but boring? [pause] What have you sacrificed?

3 · The ride — Must I pay it in full?
Heatwave, boulangerie, checked the tyres, towards the Pyrenees
Clipped in, it clicks — my version of meditation
One question: must I pay the price in full?

4 · Childhood — No. The bike. The matrix. Childhood.
The answer was my bicycle — training wheels off
The grind leaves no room to be curious
Neo on two wheels. Italy. Gateway back to childhood

5 · Instinct — Why childhood works. Go back. Don't grow up… fully.
Childhood: true character, before anyone told us
Two years untouched — yet instinct chose the bike
Lost something? Go back, pick it up. Don't grow up… fully
```
